# Investigative Documentaries — Style DNA
Learned: seed guide (refines automatically after your first Update Brain run) | teachers: fern, SLICE Full Doc, Part-Time Explorer, Beginning To Now

This niche runs in **two selectable MODES**. Pick one per script and commit.
- **MODE A — Mapped Reconstruction**: one extraordinary true event (heist, hack, manhunt, disaster) reconstructed beat-by-beat with precise places, times, and named people. The viewer feels like they're watching a map light up. 15-30 min.
- **MODE B — Forgotten Place / Slow Tragedy**: a place or vessel or town with a dramatic hidden past; patient build, dread and wonder, strong sense of physical location. 20-35 min.

## 1. Hook / cold-open
- **MODE A — the impossible fact.** Open on the single most unbelievable, verifiable fact of the story, stated plainly, then promise the reconstruction: "In [year], [person/group] did [impossible thing]. This is how they did it — step by step."
- **MODE B — the place that shouldn't exist.** Open standing (narratively) at the location today — the empty town, the rusting wreck — then flash back: "To understand how it got here, we have to go back to [year]."
- Always name a real person, place, and date in the first 30 seconds. Never open with a definition or background.

## 2. Story structure (beat map)
- **MODE A:** impossible fact (0-5%) → the players & the plan/context (5-20%) → step-by-step reconstruction with escalating stakes, each step timestamped/located (20-70%) → the unraveling: the mistake, the chase, the collapse (70-90%) → aftermath + where they are now (90-100%).
- **MODE B:** the place today (0-8%) → its golden age (8-30%) → the turning point / disaster in slow, human detail (30-70%) → the abandonment/aftermath (70-90%) → reflective return to the present (90-100%).

## 3. Voice & sentence rhythm
- Calm, precise, understated — the facts carry the drama, the narrator never hypes.
- Present tense for reconstruction scenes ("At 2:14 a.m., the alarm goes silent"); past tense for context.
- Medium declaratives punctuated by short reveal lines: "Nobody noticed." / "It worked." / "He was 17."
- Numbers are exact and frequent: times, distances, amounts of money, counts.

## 4. Retention devices
- Open a loop in the hook (how? / who? / why did it fail?) and pay it off only near the end.
- "One detail" foreshadowing: flag a small overlooked detail early, bring it back as the thing that breaks the case.
- Step-labeled escalation (the plan had three phases…) so the viewer always knows where they are.
- Person-anchoring: every abstract system (a bank, a government, a network) is represented by one named human.

## 5. Transitions
- "But there was a problem." / "Then, at [time], everything changed." / "What nobody knew was…" / "Meanwhile, [x] miles away…" / "To understand why, you need to know one thing about [topic]."

## 6. Vocabulary & tone
- Recurring diction: "reconstruct", "vanished", "untraceable", "the operation", "the breakthrough", "in broad daylight".
- AVOID: hype words ("insane", "crazy"), moralizing lectures, jokes that break tension, invented dialogue, and any unverifiable specifics — hedge honestly ("reportedly", "according to investigators").

## 7. Outro / CTA
- Land the payoff of the opening question, then one reflective beat about what the story reveals (about systems, greed, memory). Single understated sub prompt at most — no begging.

## 8. Title & thumbnail angles
- "How [ordinary person/group] [did impossible thing]" · "The [heist/hack/escape] that [broke/fooled] [big institution]" · "The Hunt for [person]" · "[Place]: The Town That [dramatic fate]".
- Thumbnail: one iconic location/object + a short irony caption; map-style graphics perform in MODE A.

## WRITER CHECKLIST
- [ ] Pick ONE mode (A Mapped Reconstruction / B Forgotten Place) and hold it start to finish.
- [ ] Cold-open on the impossible fact or the place today — a real name, place, and date in the first 30 seconds.
- [ ] Pose the spine-question in the first minute; answer it only near the end.
- [ ] Reconstruct key scenes in present tense with exact times and locations.
- [ ] Flag one small overlooked detail early and pay it off as the turning point.
- [ ] Anchor every institution or system to one named human.
- [ ] Keep the narrator calm and precise — facts carry the drama, never hype.
- [ ] Use exact numbers (times, distances, money) in every major beat.
- [ ] Hedge every uncertain claim honestly ("reportedly", "investigators believe") — invent nothing.
- [ ] End sections on questions or reveals, not summaries.
- [ ] Close by answering the opening question, then one reflective line — no cheesy CTA.
