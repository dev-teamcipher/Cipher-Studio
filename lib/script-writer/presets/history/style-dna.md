# History Documentaries — Style DNA
Learned: 2026-08-06 | corpus=8 transcripts | teachers: This Is History, The Present Past, A Day In History

This niche runs in **three selectable MODES**. Pick one per script and commit — do not blend voices mid-video.
- **MODE A — Grand Sweep** (This Is History): "The Entire History of [Empire/City/Nation]". Formal, omniscient, chronological epic. 20-30 min.
- **MODE B — Essayist/Investigator** (The Present Past): first-person, curious, morally reflective; one narrow question or a myth-debunk, not a whole civilization. Conversational.
- **MODE C — Dark Chapter** (A Day In History): a single overlooked atrocity/figure, grim and human-scale, built on named perpetrators and survivor testimony. 18-25 min.

---

## 1. Hook / cold-open
Never open with "Hi guys" or channel throat-clearing. First 15-30s must state stakes and pose the spine-question.

- **MODE A — the epithet noun-phrase.** Name the subject, stack 2-3 grand descriptors, then flip to a hook of contradiction or scale, then the spine-question, then a title-drop line.
  - "Japan — the land of the rising sun... surprisingly, this openness is recent: for 200 years its borders were sealed. How did an isolated island become one of the most open nations on earth? This is the history of Japan."
  - "Jerusalem — without doubt the most revered holy city in the world, fought over more than almost anywhere. How did one small city become the prize of three religions?"
  - Formula: **[Subject] + [grand epithets] + [surprising reversal] + "but how did..." + "This is the history of [X]."**
- **MODE B — in-medias-res scene, then the itch.** Open cold on a precise dated moment, cinematic, present tense; then step out and confess the real question.
  - "On the 6th of August 1945, six B-29 bombers took off from a tiny Pacific base... In one flash it was over. I've always wondered — why these two cities?"
  - Debunk variant: "This week I saw a new video by [big creator], and in it, he's lying to you. It looks amazing. It's also wrong."
- **MODE C — the forgotten-crime frame + content warning.** State that something huge was forgotten, name it, promise the descent.
  - "It seems impossible we'd forget the genocide of an entire people — but the Assyrian genocide shows how fickle memory is. Today on A Day In History, we look at how it unfolded."
  - If graphic: a one-line viewer warning first ("this contains descriptions of...").

## 2. Story structure (beat map)
- **MODE A (~100%):** Hook (0-5%) → deep-origins/geography (5-15%) → chaptered chronology by ruler/era/turning point, each era causing the next (15-85%) → modern payoff that resolves the opening question (85-100%). Sponsor/Substack plug is a single clause near the very start or the very end, never mid-narrative.
- **MODE B:** Cinematic cold open (0-8%) → thesis + meta-frame "this is also a story about how we write history" (8-15%) → build context step by step, one expert interview woven in (15-80%) → refuse the easy moral, leave the hard question open (80-92%) → sponsor + a warm human coda/aside (92-100%).
- **MODE C:** Forgotten-event frame (0-6%) → who the victims were + the decree/trigger (6-20%) → escalating chronological atrocities anchored to named perpetrators (20-75%) → episodes of resistance / a survivor's testimony (60-85%) → aftermath, death toll debate, denial vs recognition today (85-97%) → reflective "let us learn from this" + like/subscribe (97-100%).

## 3. Voice & sentence rhythm
- **MODE A:** long, subordinate-clause-rich sentences; third person; **no "you", no "I"**; past tense; date-and-place dense ("in 1639 the third Tokugawa... introduced sakoku"). Register: encyclopedic-dramatic, calm authority.
- **MODE B:** short punchy lines mixed with runs; heavy **"I" and "you"**; present tense for scenes; rhetorical questions the narrator then answers; casual asides and dry humor ("just another group project — except life or death"). Register: smart friend thinking aloud.
- **MODE C:** medium, plain, declarative; past tense; occasional direct survivor quotes verbatim; unflinching concrete nouns (tools, pickaxes, the lake). Register: sombre, restrained, witness.

## 4. Retention devices
- Open a loop early and pay it later ("we'll get to that", "for now"). MODE B and C both plant "but the reason is actually really grim / dark — and to explain it I have to talk about [detour]".
- Foreshadow-then-payoff detours: MODE B leaves the main subject (Tokyo) to explain Dresden firebombing, then returns.
- Cause-and-effect chaining in MODE A ("this in turn escalated into...", "as a consequence...") keeps momentum without cliffhangers.
- Named human anchors (a single survivor, a single butcher) in MODE C make abstract mass violence stick.
- Scale-comparisons as gut-punches ("almost twice US losses in Vietnam").

## 5. Transitions
- MODE A connectives: "over time however", "this in turn", "as a consequence", "that was until [year] when", "meanwhile", "although a paradigm shift occurred in [year]".
- MODE B connectives: "so", "and here's the thing", "well", "but you might be wondering what [X] has to do with [Y]", "we'll get to that".
- MODE C connectives: "things changed with", "meanwhile", "before long", "in [month year]", "one survivor of these massacres was...".
- Move between eras/sections on a **year or a named person**, almost never on a vague "later on".

## 6. Vocabulary & tone
- Recurring diction: "usher in", "de facto ruler", "flourish", "the epicenter", "at the pinnacle of its power", "atrocities", "overlooked", "genocide", "empire", "decree", epithets like "the land of the rising sun".
- AVOID: modern slang in MODE A/C; hedge-words ("kind of", "sort of") outside MODE B; moralizing verdicts in MODE C (let the facts and testimony carry it); inventing dialogue or numbers — every date, toll, and name must be real.
- MODE B is the only place for jokes, pop-culture refs (Battlefield 1942), and first-person opinion.

## 7. Outro / CTA
- **MODE A:** no hard CTA — end on a resonant thematic sentence that closes the opening question ("too big to be allowed to exist", "ceased to exist"). Any like/sub or sponsor sits as one line, detached from the finale.
- **MODE B:** dissolve the tidy conclusion ("there's endless debate — you tell me"), fold the sponsor in naturally, then a warm off-hand human aside to end on personality.
- **MODE C:** a short reflective plea ("let us learn from history so this never happens again") + plain "leave a like and subscribe as we explore more of history's darkest secrets".

## 8. Title & thumbnail angles
- **Grand Sweep:** "The Entire History of [Empire / City / Country]" — Empire and city framings out-perform generic-country ones (British Empire 4.7x, Japan 3.1x, Jerusalem 2.5x).
- **Dark Chapter:** "The Darkest Side of [X]", "The [Event] Mainstream Media Hates To Talk About", "The Event [Country] Hid From [N] People", "The [Group] That Disturbed Even the SS", often with "*WARNING Mature Audiences Only". Dark/suppressed/forbidden framings are the biggest levers (ISIS 5.5x).
- **Essayist:** a provocative correction or moral question ("Why did they really drop it here?", "[Creator] is lying to you").
- Thumbnail logic: one iconic subject + a stark word; for Dark Chapter, a warning/forbidden vibe.

## WRITER CHECKLIST
- [ ] Pick ONE mode (A Grand Sweep / B Essayist / C Dark Chapter) and hold its voice, tense, and person start to finish.
- [ ] Cold-open in the first 2 sentences: MODE A = epithet noun-phrase + reversal; MODE B = a precise dated scene; MODE C = forgotten-crime frame (+ content warning if graphic).
- [ ] State the spine-question or thesis before the 30-second mark, and make the final line answer exactly that question.
- [ ] Plant at least one open loop early ("we'll get to that") and pay it off later.
- [ ] Move between every era or section on a real year or a named person — never "later on" or "some time passed".
- [ ] Chain events by cause and effect ("this in turn...", "as a consequence...") so each beat sets up the next.
- [ ] Anchor big/abstract stakes to one concrete human (a survivor, a single ruler or butcher) and at least one scale-comparison gut-punch.
- [ ] Verify every date, name, place, and death toll against real history — invent nothing; in MODE C quote survivors verbatim, don't paraphrase into drama.
- [ ] MODE A: no "you"/"I", keep sponsor to one detached line; MODE B: use "I"/"you", one woven expert or source, allow humor; MODE C: restrained tone, let facts carry the horror.
- [ ] Land one 4-8 word thematic gut-punch as the closing sentence; attach CTA/sponsor only per this mode's outro rule.
- [ ] Title with a proven frame: "The Entire History of [Empire/City]" (A), a dark/suppressed/forbidden hook + optional WARNING (C), or a provocative correction (B).
- [ ] Never open with channel throat-clearing; earn the subscribe by delivering the promised payoff first.
